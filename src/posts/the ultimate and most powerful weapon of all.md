> "With their armies, the Volscians and the Aequi had invaded Roman territory. The consuls were sent to oppose them. As the battle wore on, the army of the Volscians, whose commander was Vettius Messius, suddenly found itself trapped between its stockades, occupied by the Romans, and the other Roman army and realizing that they had either to die or to cut their way through with their swords, Vettius spoke these words to his troops: 'There is no wall of earth or stone to stop you -- but armed men like yourselves; in courage you are as good as they -- in the ultimate and most powerful weapon of all -- desperation -- you have the advantage!' Livy calls this necessity the 'ultimate and most powerful weapon of all'.

So: one who leads should impress a necessity to perform the task at hand upon their subordinates. This is probably why companies with existential missions find it easier to recruit talent: because people who feel that same existential pressure as a company are self-motivated to perform their tasks with the aforementioned powerful weapon.

One other takeaway is that you can make yourself push harder if you radically reduce optionality. Just choose a direction to go in and go all in; don't think about other fields or things and treat your selected path as the only path.
